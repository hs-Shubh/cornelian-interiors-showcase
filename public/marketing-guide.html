1How the pieces fit together

Before touching any settings, understand the machine you're building. Each tool does one job, and they hand off to each other. Set them up in this order and nothing will be wasted.

The five tools

  • Google Business Profile (GBP) — your free listing on Google Search & Maps. This is where a Faridabad client searching “luxury interior designer near me” finds you.
  • Google Search Console (GSC) — shows how your website appears in Google Search and lets you submit your sitemap so pages get indexed.
  • Google Analytics 4 (GA4) — counts how many people visit the site and how many fill a form.
  • Meta Pixel — a tiny piece of tracking on your site that tells Facebook/Instagram who visited and who became a lead, so ads can be optimised.
  • Meta Ads — the paid campaigns that drive the right affluent people to the site.

Where leads land

When someone submits a form on the website, the lead is saved to your database and you follow up by phone / WhatsApp (wa.me/919720130734). At the same moment the site fires:

  • a GA4 event named generate_lead (counts as a conversion), and
  • a Meta Lead event (so ads learn who converts).

You don't build these — they're already wired into the site. You only supply the IDs (Sections 4 & 5).

2Google Business Profile (GBP)

Your free listing on Google Search and Google Maps. For a Faridabad studio this is the single highest-value free asset — it's how someone searching “interior designer in Faridabad” or “luxury home interiors NCR” sees your photos, reviews, and phone number, and taps to call.

Why it matters here

High-ticket clients do their homework. A polished GBP with real project photos and genuine 5-star reviews is often what tips a 50L+ prospect from “maybe” to picking up the phone. It also feeds Google Maps, which is huge for local “near me” searches in Delhi NCR.

Step-by-step: create & claim

  1. Sign in with the right Google account Use the business Gmail (cornelianexecutiveinteriors@gmail.com) so ownership stays with the business, not a personal account.
    Go to: google.com/business → Manage now
  2. Search for your business first Type “Cornelian Executive Interiors”. If a listing already exists (someone may have created it, or Google auto-generated one), choose Claim this business instead of making a duplicate. If nothing shows, click Add your business to Google.
  3. Enter the business name & category Name exactly as you use it everywhere: Cornelian Executive Interiors. Pick the primary category Interior designer.
  4. Choose how you serve customers Most interior studios pick “I deliver goods and services to my customers” (a service-area business) rather than showing a shopfront address. You can list your Faridabad office address for records but hide it, and instead show service areas (next steps).
  5. Add phone & website Phone +91 97201 30734, website https://cornelianinteriors.com. Keep the Name, Address, Phone (“NAP”) identical everywhere online — inconsistent NAP hurts local ranking.
  6. Start verification Google will offer a verification method — increasingly this is video verification (see below). Start it now; approval can take a few days.

Video verification (the common method now)

Google frequently requires a single, unedited live video that proves (a) the business is real at its location and (b) you're authorised to manage it. You must record it live in the Google Maps mobile app — pre-recorded or edited clips are rejected.

  1. Open Google Maps app on your phone Sign in with the same business Google account. Open your profile → tap Get verified → Video verification.
    Note: desktop cannot do video verification — phone only
  2. Plan your ~50-second path before filming Aim for one continuous clip of 45–55 seconds, good lighting, steady hands, no cuts.
  3. Show these things, in one take
    • A nearby street sign / building number to confirm location.
    • Your office / studio signage or entrance, then walk inside to show access.
    • Your workspace — material samples, mood boards, 3D renders on screen, furniture pieces — to prove real design activity.
    • Briefly, one business document (business card, GST/registration, a letterhead) with personal details covered.
  4. Submit and wait The video uploads straight after recording. Approval usually arrives by email within a few days. If rejected, re-shoot with clearer location proof.
Don't

Don't edit the video, don't stitch clips, and make sure the address on your profile matches the location you film — Google cross-checks Street View. Mismatches are the #1 cause of rejection.

Reviews strategy (critical for high-ticket)

  1. Ask every happy client, personally After handover, send them your review short-link (GBP gives you one under “Ask for reviews”) over WhatsApp with a one-line request.
  2. Make it specific Ask them to mention the locality and project type (e.g. “full home interior in Faridabad”) — keyword-rich reviews help local ranking.
  3. Reply to every review Thank each reviewer by name within a couple of days. Reply politely and factually to any negative review — prospects read your responses.
  4. Aim for a steady trickle For luxury, quality and recency beat quantity. A handful of detailed recent 5-star reviews with photos outperforms dozens of one-word ones.
Tip

Never buy fake reviews — Google detects and removes them and can suspend the profile. For a 50L+ studio, one suspension can cost you months of visibility.

3Google Search Console (GSC)

A free Google tool that shows how your website appears in Google Search — which queries show your site, how many clicks you get, and whether your pages are indexed. You'll also submit your sitemap here so Google discovers every page.

Step 1 — Add a property: Domain vs URL-prefix

Recommended

Domain property (cornelianinteriors.com). It covers every version — http/https, www/non-www, and all subdomains — in one place. It's the most complete view and is verified once via DNS.

Alternative

URL-prefix property tracks only one exact address (e.g. https://cornelianinteriors.com/). Use this only if you can't access DNS — it also unlocks the HTML meta-tag verification method.

Go to: search.google.com/search-console → Add property

Step 2 — Verify ownership

Option A — DNS TXT (required for a Domain property, most stable)

  1. Choose Domain & copy the TXT record GSC shows a record like google-site-verification=xxxxxxxx.
  2. Add it at your DNS host The site's DNS is managed at Vercel (or your registrar). Add a new TXT record: Name/Host = @ (or blank), Value = the string GSC gave you. Save.
    Vercel: Project → Settings → Domains / DNS → add TXT record
  3. Wait, then Verify DNS can take minutes to ~48 hours. Come back to GSC and click Verify. Leave the TXT record in place forever — if you delete it, you lose access.

Option B — HTML meta tag (for a URL-prefix property)

The site already supports a Google verification meta tag via the environment variable VITE_GSC_VERIFICATION. If you go the URL-prefix route:

  1. Copy the code from the meta-tag option GSC shows <meta name="google-site-verification" content="XXXX" />. Copy just the content value (XXXX).
  2. Set it in Vercel and redeploy Add VITE_GSC_VERIFICATION = XXXX in Vercel env vars, then redeploy. The site injects the meta tag into every page's <head>.
    Vercel: Project → Settings → Environment Variables → Add → Redeploy
  3. Click Verify in GSC
Which to pick

If you (or the developer) can edit DNS in Vercel, do the Domain property + DNS TXT — it's cleaner and covers everything. Only fall back to the meta tag if DNS is out of reach.

Step 3 — Submit your sitemap

  1. Open Sitemaps
    GSC left menu: Indexing → Sitemaps
  2. Add the sitemap URL Enter sitemap.xml (full URL https://cornelianinteriors.com/sitemap.xml — it already exists) and click Submit. Status should become Success within a day or two.

Step 4 — Reading the reports

4Google Analytics 4 (GA4)

Free tool that answers your two core questions: how many people reach the website, and how many fill a form (conversions). The site is already built to load GA4 automatically once you provide the Measurement ID.

Step 1 — Create account, property & web data stream

  1. Create a GA4 account Sign in with the business Google account.
    Go to: analytics.google.com → Admin (gear, bottom-left) → Create → Account
  2. Create a property Name it Cornelian Executive Interiors. Set reporting time zone to (GMT+05:30) India and currency to INR.
    Admin → Create → Property
  3. Add a Web data stream Choose Web, enter the site URL https://cornelianinteriors.com, name the stream, and create it.
    Admin → Data collection & modification → Data streams → Add stream → Web
  4. Copy the Measurement ID It looks like G-XXXXXXXXXX (top-right of the data stream). This is the number the site needs.
    Not the Property ID (a plain number) — you want the G- one

Step 2 — Put the Measurement ID on the site

You don't touch code

The site loads Google's gtag automatically from an environment variable. You only paste the ID and redeploy.

  1. Open Vercel env vars
    Vercel: Project → Settings → Environment Variables
  2. Add the variable Name VITE_GA_ID, value G-XXXXXXXXXX (your ID). Apply to Production (and Preview if you like). Save.
  3. Redeploy Env vars only take effect on a fresh build. Trigger a redeploy from the Deployments tab (Redeploy the latest). After it's live, open the site — GA4 should start showing you in Realtime.

Step 3 — Mark generate_lead as a Key Event (conversion)

When a form is submitted the site fires a GA4 event named generate_lead. GA4 now calls conversions “Key Events.” You must switch it on — and it only starts counting from the moment you turn it on (no backfill).

  1. Submit one test lead first Fill out a form on the live site once, so GA4 has seen the generate_lead event and it appears in the events list (can take a few hours to show).
  2. Find the event
    Admin → Data display → Events
  3. Toggle it on Find generate_lead and switch on Mark as key event (the toggle / star). Done — it now counts as a conversion.
  4. If it isn't listed yet You can pre-create it: Admin → Events → Create event, or just wait until the test lead appears, then toggle. (Up to 30 key events allowed per property.)

Step 4 — Link GA4 with Search Console

  1. Open the GSC link
    Admin → Product links → Search Console links → Link
  2. Pick your verified GSC property Choose cornelianinteriors.com, associate it with your web stream, confirm. Now organic-search data appears inside GA4.

5Meta Pixel + Facebook / Instagram Ads

The Meta Pixel (Meta now calls it a dataset) is tracking that tells Facebook & Instagram who visited your site and who became a lead, so the ad algorithm can find more people like your leads. Then you build the campaign itself.

Step 1 — Create the Pixel / dataset & get the Pixel ID

  1. Open Meta Business Suite & Events Manager Sign in / create a free Business account.
    Go to: business.facebook.com → Events Manager
  2. Connect a web data source Click Connect data → choose Web → Meta Pixel → Connect.
  3. Name it e.g. Cornelian Website Pixel (not the default name). Enter the site URL when asked.
  4. Copy the Pixel ID After Create, a long number appears — that's your Pixel ID = dataset ID. Copy it.

Step 2 — Put the Pixel ID on the site

Same pattern as GA4

No code needed — the site loads Meta's fbq from an env var.

  1. Add the env var in Vercel Name VITE_META_PIXEL_ID, value = your Pixel ID number.
    Vercel: Project → Settings → Environment Variables
  2. Redeploy Redeploy so it takes effect. The site now fires PageView on every page and a Lead event on form submit.

Step 3 — Verify events are firing

  1. Install Meta Pixel Helper Add the free Meta Pixel Helper Chrome extension. Open your live site — it should show a green tick and the Pixel ID with a PageView.
  2. Use Test Events
    Events Manager → your dataset → Test Events → enter your site URL
    Browse the site and submit a test form. You should see PageView and then Lead appear in real time.
  3. Confirm in the Overview Within a day the Overview tab shows PageView and Lead event counts — proof it's working.

Step 4 — Verify your domain (recommended)

Domain verification proves you own cornelianinteriors.com and protects your event setup. (As of June 2025 it's no longer strictly required for event measurement / optimisation — Meta's Aggregated Event Measurement is now automatic — but it's still recommended for link ownership.)

  1. Open Brand safety / Domains
    Business Settings → Brand safety → Domains → Add → enter cornelianinteriors.com
  2. Add the TXT record at your DNS Meta shows facebook-domain-verification=xxxxxxxx. Add it as a TXT record in Vercel DNS (copy exactly, no quotes/spaces), wait for propagation, then click Verify.

Step 5 — Build the Lead / Conversions campaign

  1. Create the campaign
    Go to: Ads Manager (adsmanager.facebook.com) → Create
    Choose objective Leads, then optimise for website conversions using your Pixel (drive people to the site form) rather than Instant Forms — site leads are higher-intent for high-ticket work. (You can test Instant Forms later; site-form leads usually qualify better.)
  2. Set the conversion event At the ad-set level pick your Pixel/dataset and the Lead event as the optimisation event. This tells Meta to find people likely to submit the form.
  3. Budget & schedule Start with a daily budget (see Section 6). Run continuously — don't switch ads off every few days; the algorithm needs a stable learning period.
  4. Build the audience (below)
  5. Placements Start with Advantage+ placements (Meta auto-optimises across Feed, Reels, Stories, Explore). Ensure you upload the right aspect ratios (Section 7) so the reel looks great everywhere.
  6. Publish & confirm tracking After publishing, check the first day that the Lead event is being attributed in Ads Manager.

6Ad budget & strategy for high-ticket 1 lead / month · 50L+

Your goal is unusual and it's smart: you don't want many leads — you want ~1 genuinely qualified lead a month that can become a ₹50,00,000+ project. That completely changes what “good” ad numbers look like. A high cost-per-lead is totally fine here.

Researched market ranges (India / Delhi NCR, 2025–26) — label: ranges, not quotes

Real-estate / premium-home advertising is one of the most competitive, highest-cost categories on Meta in India. Independent 2025–26 benchmarks put CPM ≈ ₹150–₹400+ for Tier-1 affluent targeting (high-income targeting runs ~25–50% above median CPM), CPC ≈ ₹3–₹8+ for real estate, and CPL ≈ ₹500–₹1,400 for ordinary property enquiries. For luxury, smaller audiences push cost-per-lead much higher — expect a qualified luxury interior enquiry to cost meaningfully more (often several thousand rupees each). Treat all figures as ranges for planning, not guarantees.

The math — why even an expensive lead is a bargain here

One project = ₹50,00,000+ in revenue. Say your gross margin is a conservative 25% → ₹12,50,000 gross profit per project. Now look at what leads can cost:

*Assumes a cautious ~10% lead-to-project close rate for high-ticket. Even spending ₹1.5 lakh in ads to land one ₹50 lakh project is a ~30× return on ad spend. This is why, for you, lead quality beats lead cost every time.

The honest truth

Luxury lead cost is high and volume is intentionally low — that's the correct strategy, not a problem. Don't panic if a lead costs ₹5,000–₹15,000. Panic only if the leads are the wrong people (renters, tiny budgets, other cities). Optimise for fit, not for cheapness.

Phased 90-day plan

  1. Month 1 — Test & learn Run 2–3 creatives (at least one reel) to the cold affluent-NCR audience at ~₹1,000/day. Goal: confirm Pixel + Lead tracking, learn which creative and audience gets the best quality replies. Judge on lead fit, not just count.
  2. Month 2 — Optimise Kill losers, double down on the winning ad. Turn on retargeting of website visitors and IG engagers (your cheapest conversions). Tighten targeting toward the localities/interests that produced real prospects.
  3. Month 3 — Scale & retarget Build a 1–3% Lookalike of leads/converters, raise budget on what works, expand geos carefully. Keep retargeting running always — it's the highest-ROI slice.

Lead-quality qualification tips

  • Add a qualifying question to the form or first WhatsApp reply: budget range, property size/type, and locality.
  • Prioritise owned homes / new possessions in premium NCR projects and full-home scopes over single-room jobs.
  • Respond within minutes — high-ticket prospects contact several studios; speed wins.
  • Track which ad / audience each closed client came from and feed that back into targeting.
  • Don't judge a campaign on 3–4 leads. Give each test ~2 weeks and enough spend to be statistically meaningful.

7The ad reel & creative

Your question: is Photoshop enough for an “extraordinary reel”?

No. Adobe Photoshop is a photo / still-image editor — it's brilliant for retouching a single render or making a poster, but it does not edit video. A reel is video: clips, cuts, motion, music, captions and timing. Photoshop can't do that. You need a proper video editor. Use Photoshop only to polish individual still frames or thumbnails that you then drop into the video editor.

Music, pacing & captions

  • Pacing: cut on the beat. Faster cuts in the details montage, slower on the hero wide shots. Total 15–30s — shorter often performs better.
  • Music: elegant, cinematic, or a tasteful trending audio — nothing loud/cheap. In ads, most people watch on mute, so it must work silently too.
  • Captions: add on-screen text/subtitles (CapCut auto-captions) since sound is often off. Keep text short and in the safe zone.
  • Colour: grade warm and rich (DaVinci/Premiere Lumetri) so interiors look luxurious, not flat phone-camera footage.
  • Quality: shoot in the best light you can, stabilise the camera (gimbal or steady hands), and always export at 1080p+.
